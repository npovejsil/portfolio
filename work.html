03 Asparagopsis Meta-Analysis Type: Paper BMC Agri. 2(4) / 2026
Fig. 03.a — Bromoform dose / forage model
Asparagopsis*

Goloja, Povejsil, Roque et al. "A meta-analysis establishes bromoform dose and forage-based models to evaluate the antimethanogenic effects of Asparagopsis spp. feed additive." Published in BMC Agriculture (2026).

Asparagopsis is a red seaweed that, when fed to cattle in small doses, dramatically reduces their methane emissions. This paper consolidates results from dozens of individual trials into dose-response and forage-adjusted models — work that directly supports regulatory approval pathways and carbon credit methodologies for the livestock industry.

Conducted during my time at Symbrosia, where I also contributed ROI analyses used in investor pitches and modeled emissions reductions for commercial deployment scenarios.

02 HydroScale Type: Capstone UCB / MIDS / 12.2024
Fig. 02.a — Pipeline architecture
HydroScale*

Forecasting Water Usage Efficiency in Data Centers. AI compute is scaling faster than the water infrastructure supporting it. HydroScale is a forecasting model for data-center Water Usage Efficiency (WUE) that pairs weather, workload, and facility data to project operational water demand — a first step toward giving operators, regulators, and surrounding communities a common language for how thirsty this infrastructure really is.

Built as a MIDS capstone, the project combined public EIA/EPA datasets with Iowa Environmental Mesonet weather data. We ingested, staged, and simulated WUE across on-site and off-site scenarios, then served forecasts to a dashboard for data-center operators.

Pipeline: AWS S3 + EC2 ingestion of EIA, EPA, and IEM sources → Spark preprocessing & WUE simulation on EMR/PySpark → SageMaker on-site and off-site forecast models → Flask model serving to data-center operators.

01 GeoNdxR Type: Package UW eScience / DSSG / 08.2024
Fig. 01.a — Composite environmental indices
GeoNdxR*

Open-source software for environmental indices. GeoNdxR is an R package and mapping tool that lets communities and policymakers build composite environmental indices from public datasets. We used it to help the EPA and Water Research Foundation surface places where vulnerable populations overlap with infrastructure gaps — the first step toward identifying water reuse opportunities at the community scale.

The tool turns what was previously a bespoke GIS analysis into a reproducible workflow: pick your indicators, weight them, and generate maps that can be handed directly to local decision-makers. Findings were presented to 125+ attendees in a public webcast rated "Exceptional."
